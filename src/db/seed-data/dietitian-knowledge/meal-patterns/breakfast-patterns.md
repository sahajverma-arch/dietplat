# Breakfast Meal Patterns

## One cereal item, not a spread

A realistic Indian breakfast plate centers on a single cereal-based item — a stuffed or plain paratha, a poha/upma-style preparation, idli/dosa in the south, or a porridge like oats or dalia. It is not a spread of three or four different dishes; households eat one main breakfast item, not a buffet.

## A dairy accompaniment, almost always

Curd, raita, or a glass of milk/chai usually accompanies the cereal item. A porridge-style breakfast (plain oats, dalia) is the exception — it is not conventionally topped with a separate fat like ghee the way a paratha is, since the porridge itself is already the complete, moist dish.

## Eggs, when present, pair with a plain base

When a breakfast includes egg (omelette, bhurji, boiled egg), it commonly pairs with a plain, unstuffed flatbread rather than a rich stuffed paratha — two distinct savory-and-fatty elements stacked together (a stuffed paratha plus a cooked egg dish) reads as a heavier plate than a typical breakfast, whereas a plain roti alongside egg is the more common everyday pairing.

## Breakfast is the lightest of the day's full meals

Relative to lunch and dinner, breakfast portions run smaller and simpler. A generated breakfast that matches lunch or dinner in size or dish count will look wrong to a dietitian reviewing the week, even if the macros land correctly.
