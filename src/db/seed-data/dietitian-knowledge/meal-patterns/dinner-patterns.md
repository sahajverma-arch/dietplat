# Dinner Meal Patterns

## The day's second substantial meal

Dinner, like lunch, needs a real cereal or rice base plus a vegetable and/or pulse — it is the second of the two meals the day is actually built around, not a lighter wind-down. A dinner made mostly of soup, salad, or a smoothie will leave the day's calorie and carbohydrate targets unmet even if breakfast and lunch were on track.

## Non-vegetarian dishes belong at dinner

For a non-vegetarian client, the day's meat or fish dish is conventionally served at dinner, not lunch — dinner is treated as its own protein-and-side course built around that dish, while lunch stays on the everyday dal-and-sabzi pattern. When a meal is built around a meat or fish dish, it is typically not also carrying a separate vegetable curry alongside it — the meat dish and its accompanying starch are the meal, without a second competing vegetable dish crowding the plate.

## Curd or raita, but rarely milk

A katori of curd or raita is a common dinner accompaniment, much like at lunch. A full glass of milk, by contrast, is not conventionally taken right alongside dinner — where milk appears in a day's plan at all, it is more of a standalone item than a dinner-table addition.

## Dinner should feel distinct from lunch, not a repeat

Across a week, dinner's cereal and vegetable choices should vary rather than mirroring the same day's lunch — real households don't eat the identical dish twice in one day.
