# Mid-Morning Meal Patterns

## A single piece of fruit, nothing more

Mid-morning is the smallest slot of the day. In practice it is almost always one fruit, eaten on its own — an apple, a banana, a bowl of papaya. It is not a place for a second breakfast, a savory snack, or a multi-item spread; a client's real routine at this hour is a short break, not a sit-down meal.

## Never a different second fruit stacked in

When a day's fruit total needs to be larger, the realistic adjustment is a bigger serving of the same fruit, not adding a second, different fruit alongside it. Real households don't plate an apple and a banana together for a mid-morning break; they eat more of one thing.

## No cooked or composed dishes at this slot

A cooked dish, a sandwich, or anything requiring assembly does not belong at mid-morning — it reads as a meal, not a break, and pulls attention away from lunch arriving a short time later. If a fruit-only day feels nutritionally light, that gap is meant to be closed elsewhere (lunch, dinner), not by turning mid-morning into a second meal.
