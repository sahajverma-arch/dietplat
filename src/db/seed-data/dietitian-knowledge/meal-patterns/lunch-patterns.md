# Lunch Meal Patterns

## Lunch needs a real cereal or rice base

Lunch must be built around a substantial cereal or rice dish — roti, rice, or a pulao/biryani-style preparation — not just a dal and a vegetable eaten alone. A day's carbohydrate and calorie total depends heavily on this base; a lunch made only of light items (soup, salad, a small snack) will fall well short of the day's targets even if each item individually looks reasonable.

## One vegetable dish, not two competing ones

A realistic lunch plate carries one cooked vegetable preparation, occasionally paired with a raw salad on the side. Two different cooked vegetable dishes stacked into the same meal is not how an everyday plate is composed — that combination only appears as a genuinely named two-vegetable dish (like a potato-and-cauliflower preparation), not as two unrelated vegetables served side by side.

## A pulse or protein anchor

Alongside the cereal and vegetable, lunch typically carries a dal, a legume preparation, or — for non-vegetarian clients — a meat/fish dish. For a non-vegetarian client, lunch conventionally stays on the vegetarian-style dal-and-sabzi pattern, with the day's meat or fish dish reserved for dinner rather than doubling up at both meals.

## Curd or raita rounds out the plate

A katori of curd or raita is a common, near-default addition to lunch — it is not unusual for it to appear every day, unlike a second vegetable dish, which should stay occasional.
