# Punjabi Regional Diet Patterns

## Wheat-forward, and richer than the wider North Indian pattern

Punjabi cooking shares North India's wheat-roti backbone but runs noticeably richer — more generous use of ghee and butter, and dairy showing up in more places across the day, not just as a side accompaniment. A Punjabi week should read as hearty rather than restrained.

## Makkhan and ghee as everyday, not occasional, fats

Butter (makkhan) and ghee are used generously in everyday Punjabi cooking — tempering dal, finishing a sabzi, or spread on a roti — rather than being reserved for special occasions. This is one of the clearest markers that distinguishes Punjabi cooking from a lighter regional profile.

## Sarson da saag and makki di roti as a real seasonal identity

The mustard-greens-and-cornbread combination is a genuinely distinct, well-known Punjabi winter dish pairing — a strong example of a region-specific combination that shouldn't be substituted with a generic wheat roti when the season and region call for it.

## Lassi and buttermilk as everyday drinks

A glass of lassi or buttermilk is a common Punjabi accompaniment, especially around a heavier meal — it plays a similar cooling role to curd elsewhere, but is more likely to be a drink in its own right here than just a side katori.
