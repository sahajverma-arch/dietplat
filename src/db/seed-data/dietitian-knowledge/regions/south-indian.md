# South Indian Regional Diet Patterns

## Rice, not wheat, is the everyday staple

Rice — plain, or fermented into idli/dosa batter — is the backbone of most meals, with wheat-based breads appearing far less often than in the north. A South Indian week should be rice-forward rather than roti-forward.

## Fermented breakfast dishes are a real regional identity

Idli, dosa, and similar fermented rice-and-lentil preparations are a distinctly South Indian breakfast pattern, typically served with a coconut-based chutney and/or a thin lentil-vegetable stew (sambar-style) rather than curd the way a North Indian breakfast might be.

## Coconut and curry leaf define the flavor base

Coconut (fresh, grated, or as oil) and curry leaf tempering run through much of South Indian cooking, distinct from the ghee-and-dairy-forward profile further north. Coconut-based chutneys and coconut-tempered vegetable preparations are common, everyday items, not occasional specialties.

## Curd rice as a common meal-closer

A curd-rice preparation, eaten at the end of a meal or as a light dinner on its own, is a recognizable South Indian pattern — a cooling, simple dish distinct from the raita-as-side-accompaniment pattern seen elsewhere.
