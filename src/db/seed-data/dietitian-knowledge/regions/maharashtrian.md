# Maharashtrian Regional Diet Patterns

## A genuine mix of both major grains

Unlike regions that lean firmly toward one staple, Maharashtrian cooking uses both wheat (roti/bhakri) and rice regularly, along with millets like jowar and bajra for bhakri — a Maharashtrian week can rotate across all of these rather than settling on just one cereal.

## Peanut and coconut both feature prominently

Maharashtrian cooking draws on both peanut-based and coconut-based preparations depending on the sub-region and dish — groundnut is a common thickener and garnish, alongside coconut in chutneys and some vegetable preparations. This dual influence is part of what distinguishes it from its immediate neighbors.

## A distinctive tangy-spicy profile with kokum and tamarind

Souring agents like kokum and tamarind, combined with a notably spicier chili profile than some other regions, give Maharashtrian food a recognizable sharp, tangy character across dals and vegetable curries.

## Poha and other light, savory breakfasts

A flattened-rice preparation (poha), often with peanuts and a light tempering, is a well-known everyday Maharashtrian breakfast — light, savory, and quick, distinct from the richer stuffed-flatbread breakfasts seen further north.
