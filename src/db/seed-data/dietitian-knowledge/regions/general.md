# Pan-Indian / General Diet Patterns

## Dishes with no strong regional identity

Plain rice, a basic dal (moong, toor, chana), the most common everyday vegetables (potato, cauliflower, spinach, okra), and a plain wheat roti appear across almost every region's table, regardless of that region's own distinct specialties. These general items are what fill the gaps around a region's more distinctive dishes, not a replacement for regional identity.

## Used as the always-eligible fallback pool

When a client's specific regional cuisine doesn't have enough named dishes to fill every slot of a week, these general, pan-Indian staples are what fill the remainder — they are common and familiar enough to appear anywhere without reading as out of place, even in a week that's otherwise built around a specific region's identity.

## Not a substitute for genuine regional identity

A week built entirely from general staples, with no region-specific dishes at all, misses the point of asking for a specific cuisine — general items should round out a week's variety, not be the majority of it, whenever real regional dishes are available for that client's cuisine.
