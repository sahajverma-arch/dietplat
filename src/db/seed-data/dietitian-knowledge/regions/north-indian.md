# North Indian Regional Diet Patterns

## Wheat is the staple grain

Roti and paratha, made from wheat flour, are the everyday cereal base at most meals — rice appears too, but as an occasional addition rather than the daily staple the way it is further south or east. A North Indian week should lean noticeably roti/paratha-forward rather than splitting evenly between wheat and rice.

## Dairy runs through the whole day

Ghee, paneer, and curd all show up frequently — a ghee tempering on dal, paneer as a common protein source for vegetarian clients, and curd or raita as a near-daily table accompaniment. This is a genuinely dairy-heavy regional pattern compared to, for example, a coconut-forward South Indian one.

## Stuffed parathas as a common breakfast identity

A stuffed flatbread — filled with potato, cauliflower, radish, or paneer depending on the season — is a distinctly North Indian breakfast pattern, usually paired with curd or a raita rather than eaten plain.

## Legume variety at lunch and dinner

Rajma, chana, and several dal varieties (moong, toor, masoor) rotate through lunch and dinner regularly — North Indian home cooking doesn't lean on just one dal, and a week that repeats the same dal every day understates the real variety available in this cuisine.
