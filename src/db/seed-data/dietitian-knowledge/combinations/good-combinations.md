# Good Meal Combinations

## Cereal, pulse, and vegetable together

The classic roti-or-rice plus dal plus sabzi combination works precisely because each element plays a different role — the cereal for bulk and carbohydrate, the dal for protein, the vegetable for fiber and volume. This three-part structure is the single most reliable "good combination" pattern across nearly every region.

## Curd alongside a spiced main dish

A cooling curd or raita paired with a more heavily spiced main dish is a well-established, complementary pairing — it balances the heat of the main dish rather than competing with it, which is part of why curd recurs so often as an accompaniment rather than a standalone item.

## Matching the cereal to the vegetable's texture

A dry, spiced vegetable dish pairs naturally with a roti (which can be used to scoop it), while a wetter, gravy-based curry pairs more naturally with rice (which absorbs the gravy) — this textural matching is a real, practical reason certain cereal-vegetable pairings recur more often than others.

## A protein-forward dish with a fiber-forward vegetable

Pairing a denser protein source (paneer, a legume-heavy dal, meat/fish) with a lighter, high-fiber vegetable rounds out a meal well — the vegetable adds volume and micronutrients without competing with the protein dish for richness.
