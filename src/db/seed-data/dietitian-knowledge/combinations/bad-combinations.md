# Bad Meal Combinations

## Two competing starchy mains in one meal

Rice and a stuffed paratha served together in the same meal is a real mismatch — both are trying to be the meal's main starch, and a real household plate doesn't double up like this. One cereal base per meal is the norm; if a second grain-based item shows up, it should replace the first, not sit alongside it.

## Two fried or heavy items stacked together

A fried snack alongside a rich, ghee-heavy main dish makes for an unrealistically indulgent single meal — real everyday eating spaces these out rather than combining them. If one element of a meal is already rich, the rest of the plate should be lighter to balance it, not equally heavy.

## Two separate cooked vegetable dishes in one meal

Two different cooked vegetables served side by side (rather than as one genuinely named combination dish) is not a typical everyday plate — real sabzi is overwhelmingly single-vegetable, with a second vegetable item only appearing occasionally as a distinct, deliberately mixed dish, not as a default pairing.

## Dessert-like sweetness stacked right beside a rich savory dish

A sweet dish served alongside an already-rich, heavily-spiced savory main is an unusual combination — sweets are more typically a standalone course or occasional addition, not paired directly against a rich main in the same sitting.

## A tea-time or breakfast-scale item filling a lunch or dinner slot

A soup, a smoothie, or a light snack item used as the entirety of lunch or dinner is a mismatch of scale — those items belong at mid-morning or evening, and using them to fill a main-meal slot leaves the meal both nutritionally thin and unrealistic as something a client would actually eat as lunch or dinner.
