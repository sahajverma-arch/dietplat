# Protein Distribution Principles

## Spread protein across the day rather than backloading it

A day's protein target is better served by having a real protein source at breakfast, lunch, and dinner (and often a snack) than by concentrating almost all of it into a single large dinner — a day that's protein-light all morning and protein-heavy only at night is a less balanced structure than one that distributes it.

## Different diet types lean on different anchors

For a vegetarian client, dal, paneer, and curd are the day's recurring protein anchors. For an eggetarian client, eggs join that list. For a non-vegetarian client, dal and curd still carry much of breakfast and lunch's protein, with meat or fish typically anchoring dinner specifically rather than every meal.

## Curd and dal are quietly doing a lot of protein work

Because curd and dal appear so frequently across meals already (as accompaniments, not just as dedicated protein courses), they contribute meaningfully to a day's protein total on their own — this is part of why a vegetarian day doesn't need an unusually large number of dedicated "protein dishes" to hit a real protein target.

## A protein source at breakfast, specifically

Breakfast is the meal most likely to be protein-light by default (a plain cereal item with just curd) — a genuine protein anchor there (paneer, egg, a thicker curd, or a legume-based item like a besan pancake) makes a meaningful difference to how evenly the day's protein lands.
