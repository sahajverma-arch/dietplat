# Dietitian Reasoning — Worked Examples

## A Gujarati vegetarian client on a fat-loss target

For lunch, the instinct might be to reach for a besan-based fried snack because Gujarati cuisine offers so many strong options in that category — but on a fat-loss target, that reads as one of the "avoid stacking two rich items" cases if the rest of the meal is also on the heavier side. The better choice is a thin dal, a dry (not fried) vegetable preparation, and a moderate roti portion — keeping the meal's one indulgent element, if any, to the evening snack instead of lunch.

## A Punjabi non-vegetarian client's dinner

Given Punjab's genuinely rich, ghee-forward cooking style, it's tempting to pair a butter-based chicken preparation with a stuffed paratha for dinner — but that combination stacks two rich elements (a buttery main dish and a stuffed, fatty bread) in one meal. A plain roti alongside the chicken dish, with the vegetable component skipped in favor of a simple raita, keeps the meal within a realistic richness range while still reflecting the region's real character.

## A South Indian client's breakfast on a muscle-gain target

A single idli or dosa serving is a light, appropriate breakfast for a maintenance or fat-loss target, but it's protein- and calorie-light for a muscle-gain target on its own. Rather than abandoning the region's real breakfast identity, the reasoning is to keep the dosa but pair it with a more generous sambar portion and a coconut chutney, plus a side of curd — staying within the region's authentic pattern while still landing closer to the higher target.

## A Bengali client's weekly fish frequency

Because fish is a genuine everyday Bengali staple rather than an occasional dish, a week that includes it only once undersells the region's real identity — the reasoning here is to let a fish preparation anchor two or three dinners across the week (not every single day, to preserve variety), rather than defaulting to generic chicken or paneer dishes that don't reflect this region's actual eating pattern.
