# Raw vs. Cooked Weight Guidelines

## What's plated is cooked, or as-served, weight

When a dietitian describes how much of a dish a client should eat, that figure is the weight of the dish as it actually sits on the plate — after cooking, and including any liquid, oil, or gravy it was prepared with — not the weight of the dry, raw ingredients that went into making it.

## Raw and cooked weight are not interchangeable

A dry lentil or rice measured before cooking weighs meaningfully less than the same food after it absorbs water during cooking — describing a portion in raw-ingredient terms when the client is actually eating the cooked dish would understate what's really on the plate, and vice versa.

## This distinction matters most for cereals and pulses

Rice, dal, and similar dishes change weight the most between their raw and cooked states, which is why this distinction is worth being explicit about for those categories in particular — a vegetable dish or a piece of fruit doesn't have the same dramatic before-and-after difference.
