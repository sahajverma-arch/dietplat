# Typical Serving Expectations

## Dal and curd are measured in katoris

A dal, a vegetable curry, or a raita is naturally described and portioned in katoris (a small bowl) — one katori of dal alongside two to three rotis is a completely ordinary lunch or dinner serving, not an unusually small one.

## Roti and rice have their own natural units

Roti is counted in pieces (two to three per meal is typical for an adult), while rice is more naturally described as a cup or a small bowl rather than counted individually. A dietitian describing a plate to a client uses these natural units, not raw gram figures.

## A single fruit serving is "one" of that fruit

A mid-morning fruit serving is naturally described as one whole fruit (one apple, one banana) or a small bowl for a chopped fruit like papaya — not fractions or unusual multiples, and not mixed with a second different fruit to make up a serving.

## Snacks and nuts are described in small, familiar units

A handful of nuts, a small cup of roasted makhana, or a spoonful of seeds are the natural way an evening snack is described — these are inherently small portions relative to a full meal, and should read that way in how a plan is communicated.
