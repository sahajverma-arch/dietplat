# Meal Variety Principles

## Real households repeat a rotation, not force constant novelty

A believable week doesn't serve a completely different dish at every single meal — real eating settles into a rotation of familiar dishes that recur every few days. A dish showing up twice across a week is normal and expected, not a sign of a poorly varied plan.

## But the exact same dish shouldn't dominate the week

At the other extreme, a single dish repeating on most or all days of the week reads as a planning shortcut rather than a realistic diet — some genuine rotation across the week's dal, vegetable, and cereal choices is expected, even while allowing individual dishes to repeat once or twice.

## Vary the vegetable and dal more than the cereal

Roti or rice repeating as the cereal base most days is completely normal — that's the expected staple. The variety a client actually notices and appreciates is in which dal and which vegetable dish accompany that staple from day to day, so rotation effort is better spent there than on forcing cereal variety for its own sake.

## Don't force novelty at the cost of realism

A technically "more varied" week that reaches for unusual or rarely-eaten dishes just to avoid repetition is a worse outcome than a week that repeats a genuinely common, well-liked dish — familiarity and realism matter more than maximizing distinct dish count.
