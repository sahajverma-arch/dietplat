# Common Indian Eating Habits

## Chai punctuates the day, not just breakfast

Multiple small tea breaks across the day — mid-morning, afternoon, evening — are a normal, deeply ingrained habit in most Indian households, not a sign of excessive snacking. A plan should work with this habit (giving tea a real place at appropriate slots) rather than trying to design it away.

## A mid-afternoon or evening snack is expected, not optional

Beyond the five conventional meal slots, a small snack habit around tea time is close to universal — this is exactly what the evening slot's light-snack pattern is meant to reflect, rather than being an unnecessary addition to the day.

## Festival and weekend meals run heavier

It's a normal, widely-shared pattern for a Sunday or festival meal to be noticeably richer or more elaborate than an ordinary weekday meal — this isn't a lapse in an otherwise consistent diet, it's a real and expected part of how many households actually eat, and a plan doesn't need to treat every single day as identical in richness.

## Eating together, and around family mealtimes

Meals are often eaten together as a household at broadly consistent times each day, which is part of why a plan's meal-slot timing should stay realistic and consistent across the week rather than shifting unpredictably.
